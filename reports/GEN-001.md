# GEN-001 — Held-Out Cross-Generator Generalization

**Status: EXECUTED 2026-08-15.** Design: [docs/experiments/GEN-001.md](../docs/experiments/GEN-001.md).
Decision record: [DEC-019](../docs/decisions/DEC-019-gen001-generator-selection.md).

## 1. Research question

Does EXP-003A's frozen detector (features + L2 logistic regression,
trained only on human vs. Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct `full_ai` essays) learn
characteristics of AI-generated writing that generalize to a second,
genuinely different generator, or is it primarily learning a
Qwen-specific fingerprint? Scope: `full_ai` only, this first pass (see
docs/experiments/GEN-001.md §E for why `ai_assisted` is deferred).

## 4. Generation methodology

For each of the 23 human test essays, `scripts/run_gen001_generate.py`
looked up the essay's original PERSUADE prompt (`assignment`) and task
type, then called Phi-3.5-mini-instruct with the **same**
`FULL_GENERATION_INSTRUCTION`/`FULL_GENERATION_META` templates, the
same `temperature=0.85`/`top_p=0.95`, and the same length-budgeting
(`generation_utils.budget_max_new_tokens`,
`truncate_to_word_budget`) already validated for Qwen's `full_ai`
category — the only variable changed is which model generates the
text. Target word count = the human essay's own word count (matching
EXP-003A's own `full_ai` target-length convention).

## 10. Feature-group comparison (Qwen vs. Phi, side by side)

| Feature group | Qwen test accuracy | Phi accuracy | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combined (primary, frozen threshold) | 97.8% (45/46) | 97.8% (45/46) | **0** — identical |
| Combined (@ 0.5) | 100.0% (46/46) | 100.0% (46/46) | **0** — identical |
| Stylometric-only | 100.0% (46/46) | 100.0% (46/46) | **0** — perfect transfer |
| LM-only | 84.8% (39/46) | 69.6% (32/46) | **−15.2 points** — real degradation |

**Not combined into one number, per instruction.** The primary
(combined) and stylometric-only detectors show **strong, essentially
complete transfer** to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct. The LM-only detector — the
group DEC-004 already found weakest and least reliable on Qwen data
across three prior experiments — degrades further on the held-out
generator, most visibly in `full_ai` recall: **23/23 (100%) on Qwen's
own `lm_only` test split vs. 13/23 (56.5%, 95% CI [36.8%, 74.4%]) on
Phi**, i.e. the LM-only model on Phi essays performs only modestly above
what a coin flip would achieve on this specific subset.

## 11. Feature-distribution analysis (human vs. Qwen full_ai vs. Phi full_ai)

Full 29-feature table computed with no cherry-picking (see
`experiments/GEN-001/results.json`'s `feature_distribution_all_29`).
Selected features, means per group:

**Lexical diversity / repetition** (stylometric, drove the near-perfect
transfer):

| Feature | human | Qwen full_ai | Phi full_ai |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stylo_type_token_ratio` | 0.460 | 0.752 | 0.628 |
| `stylo_repeated_bigram_ratio` | 0.244 | 0.028 | 0.097 |
| `stylo_repeated_trigram_ratio` | 0.097 | 0.003 | 0.024 |
| `stylo_repeated_sentence_opening_ratio` | 0.138 | 0.006 | 0.029 |

Both generators sit clearly on the same side of human writing on every
one of these — higher lexical diversity, far less repetition — which is
exactly the signal the stylometric-only model uses. Phi's values are
less extreme than Qwen's (closer to human on repetition specifically)
but never cross back toward human territory, which is consistent with
the transfer being perfect rather than merely partial.

**Sentence rhythm / length / punctuation / style**:

| Feature | human | Qwen full_ai | Phi full_ai |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stylo_sentence_length_cv` | 0.515 | 0.360 | 0.316 |
| `stylo_mean_avg_word_length` | 4.304 | 5.925 | 5.959 |
| `stylo_mean_char_count` | 85.222 | 121.993 | 114.609 |
| `stylo_mean_punctuation_count` | 1.424 | 2.727 | 2.701 |
| `stylo_mean_dependency_depth` | 5.015 | 6.497 | 5.997 |

Same pattern: both generators write with lower sentence-length
variability, longer words, and more punctuation per sentence than
human essays — again, same direction, similar magnitude, for both
generators.

**LM-derived (predictability) features**:

| Feature | human | Qwen full_ai | Phi full_ai |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lm_mean_perplexity` | 76.109 | 35.762 | 28.629 |
| `lm_mean_mean_log_prob` | −3.633 | −3.386 | −3.161 |
| `lm_mean_predictability_delta` | 0.061 | 0.079 | **0.051** |
| `lm_mean_token_count` (length proxy, not predictability — EXP-003B-R1) | 21.923 | 23.753 | 22.593 |

Perplexity and mean log-prob move in the **same direction** for both
generators (both more predictable than human, Phi even more so than
Qwen by distilgpt2's own measure) — if anything this would predict
*better*, not worse, LM-only transfer. But `lm_mean_predictability_delta`
**reverses sign relative to human between the two generators**: Qwen
full_ai sits *above* human (0.079 > 0.061) while Phi full_ai sits
*below* human (0.051 < 0.061). This is a plausible, disclosed
explanation (not asserted as proven causal) for why the LM-only model —
fit on Qwen's pattern, where higher delta was associated with
`machine` — loses discriminative power on Phi: one of its six input
features moved in the direction historically associated with `human`.

## 12. Probability / score distribution

Primary combined model's `P(machine)` scores:

| | n | mean | median | std | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| human (both comparisons — same essays/scores) | 23 | 0.176 | 0.152 | 0.099 | 0.042 | 0.490 |
| Qwen full_ai (reference) | 23 | 0.789 | 0.799 | 0.089 | 0.571 | 0.919 |
| Phi full_ai | 23 | 0.748 | 0.766 | 0.055 | 0.646 | 0.833 |

Phi's `full_ai` score distribution is slightly lower-mean and
**noticeably tighter** (std 0.055 vs. Qwen's 0.089) than Qwen's — Phi
essays cluster more consistently in the 0.65–0.83 range, vs. Qwen's
wider 0.57–0.92 spread. Critically, **there is zero overlap** between
the human score range (max 0.490) and the Phi `full_ai` score range
(min 0.646) — the frozen 0.47 threshold sits cleanly in the gap for
both generators, which is why accuracy is identical despite the shift
in distribution shape.

## 13. Failure cases

**Exactly one error** in the primary combined model's held-out
evaluation, and it is **not a Phi-related failure**:

`302DC21A6DEE__human` (score 0.49, just above the 0.47 threshold) was
misclassified as `machine`. This is the **same family** misclassified
in EXP-003A, EXP-003B, and EXP-003C — now a **fourth** independent
experiment in which this one human essay (elevated lexical diversity,
per prior reports) is flagged. Because this is a human-side false
positive using the identical frozen scores as EXP-003A's own test
result (same essay, same model, same features — nothing about it
depends on which generator's `full_ai` essays are in the comparison
set), it is evidence about this one atypical essay, not about
cross-generator transfer.

**Zero Phi `full_ai` essays were misclassified** by the primary model
at either threshold tested (0.47 or 0.5) — 23/23 correct.

For the (already-weaker) LM-only model, 10 of 23 Phi `full_ai` essays
were misclassified (recall 56.5%) — these are genuine, generator-linked
failures, consistent with §11's `predictability_delta` sign-reversal
finding.

## 14. Generalization interpretation (per the framework fixed in advance)

This is **Outcome C — mixed transfer**, not strong transfer or
collapse: the primary (combined) detector and the stylometric-only
detector show **essentially complete transfer** (identical accuracy,
zero-overlap score separation) to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct; the LM-only
detector — already the weakest, least-trusted group across three prior
Qwen-only experiments (DEC-004) — **degrades further** on the held-out
generator specifically on `full_ai` recall.

This is genuine evidence that **the measured stylometric signal (lower
repetition, higher lexical diversity, longer words/more punctuation,
different dependency-depth profile than human writing) generalizes
beyond Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct specifically** to at least one other,
architecturally distinct, differently-trained instruction-tuned model.
It is not evidence of universal generalization — Phi-3.5-mini-instruct
is one specific, well-resourced, RLHF-instruction-tuned model, and
these particular stylometric regularities (over-diverse vocabulary,
under-repetition relative to humans) are a plausible common byproduct
of how such models are trained/decoded, not a proven law about all
possible generators.

The LM-only degradation should **not** be read as fully resolving
DEC-004 in either direction: it is now four independent findings (this
one included) all pointing the same way — the LM feature group is the
least reliable component of this detector — but this experiment
specifically shows that unreliability compounds across generators
too, which is new information, not just a repeat of the earlier
essay-level/sentence-level results.

## 15. Limitations

- **n=23** Phi `full_ai` essays (46 total with human) — small, per the
  design's own stated limitation; confidence intervals reported
  throughout, not bare percentages.
- **A single alternative generator.** This establishes generalization
  to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct specifically, not to "AI-generated text" in
  general — the same scope discipline applied to every EXP-003 report.
- **`ai_assisted` cross-generator generalization remains completely
  untested** — GEN-001's first pass is `full_ai` only, per the
  pre-registered design (docs/experiments/GEN-001.md §E).
- **Both models are instruction-tuned, RLHF-aligned, well-resourced
  models from major labs.** A smaller, non-instruction-tuned, or
  differently-trained generator (e.g. a base/completion model, or one
  trained with different decoding defaults) might show a different
  result — untested here.
- The one human-side error (§13) is a **pre-existing, generator-
  independent** detector quirk with one specific essay, not a new
  finding about Phi — but it does mean this evaluation set's ceiling
  was already capped at 45/46 for the combined model before Phi was
  ever generated.
- The `predictability_delta` sign-reversal explanation (§11) for the
  LM-only degradation is a **plausible association**, not a proven
  mechanism — six features move together in the LM-only model, and no
  single-feature ablation was run to isolate this claim further (would
  require additional modeling not authorized in this pass).

## 16. Conclusion

GEN-001 found **mixed transfer**: the detector's primary, currently-
deployed configuration (combined features, L2 logistic regression) and
its stylometric-only component generalize essentially perfectly from
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct on this small held-out
set, while its already-weakest component (the LM-only feature group)
degrades further on the new generator. This is genuine, if scoped,
evidence that the detector's real-world signal is dominated by
stylometric regularities that are not unique to one generation model —
directly relevant to, but not a full resolution of, this project's
standing "is this general AI-writing signal or a Qwen fingerprint"
question (§1). The LM instrument's contribution remains the least
trustworthy part of the system, now across four independent
experimental angles.

GPU-equipped Linux box) would reasonably make a different device
